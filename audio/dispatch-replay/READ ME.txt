DISPATCH REPLAY
Makes finished recordings playable, and puts them back on the air.

START
  Double-click "Dispatch Replay.command". The control page opens in Chrome at http://localhost:8765
  The first time, macOS may refuse to open it: right-click the file, choose Open, then Open again.
  Leave the Terminal window open while it runs. Close it to stop.

  Needs ffmpeg. If the page says it is missing:  brew install ffmpeg   (or: sudo port install ffmpeg)

FIRST STEPS
  1. Settings → Recordings: list the folders that hold your recordings, one per line.
     For example the 8TB drive's recordings folder and SSD1's "down 1" folder. Subfolders count.
  2. A recording becomes playable and airable once it has stopped growing for 90 seconds,
     so a file OBS is still writing, or one still copying in, is never aired half-finished.
     Files you drop on the page land in "Replay inbox" and are ready at once.
  3. Press Play on any recording to watch or hear it on the page. Queue, Next and Air now line it up.

TWO WAYS TO AIR
  Through OBS (default)
    In OBS: Tools → WebSocket Server Settings → Enable WebSocket server (port 4455).
    Show Connect Info → copy the password into Settings → OBS.
    Going on the air builds a "Dispatch Replay" scene in OBS. That scene holds a media source and,
    behind audio-only recordings, Days on End as a browser source. Replay then switches OBS to that
    scene and plays the queue one recording after another. OBS keeps streaming as it always does,
    so Dispatch's 11:45 cycling carries on untouched. Going off the air puts your previous scene back.

  On its own (direct)
    Paste your stream URL with its key (rtmp://a.rtmp.youtube.com/live2/xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx) into
    Settings → Direct. Add several to stream to more than one place, and/or a folder to save a copy.
    Replay streams with ffmpeg: OBS can run the live show at the same time, or stay closed.
    Every 11 h 45 min each output reconnects for a fresh archive, while the recording keeps playing.
    Audio-only recordings get a spectrum, scope or waveform picture, or a video loop or image you choose.
    With nothing queued it airs slow generated visuals, so the stream never drops.

WHAT AIRS NEXT
  Your queue first. Each recording that just finished joins the end of the queue (you can turn this off).
  When the queue runs dry it picks a finished recording at random (OS entropy), skipping anything
  aired in the last 48 hours.

KEEPS GOING
  If OBS quits or restarts, Replay reconnects and picks the recording up where it was.
  If the computer or the program restarts, it goes back on the air where it left off.
  While on the air it keeps the Mac awake.

DAYS ON END
  "Days On End.html" is the generative backdrop. It is the same piece as before, now with the broadcast
  cycle built in: inside OBS it starts a new cycle (fresh tuning, tempo and odds) whenever OBS starts
  streaming, and at least every 11:45. In a browser add ?cycle=11:45 to the address for the same.

FILES
  replay_data/   settings, the recordings index, the queue, thumbnails and replay.log
  Settings → Other computers lets the MacBook open the page over your network with an access code.
