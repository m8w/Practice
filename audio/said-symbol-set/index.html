SAID

A sign language for talking with language models about mathematics, science and philosophy. Every sign speaks on five channels at once, has a plain-text code a model reads exactly, can be drawn in your own hand from the notebooks, and sits inside an axiom system whose consistency is checked, in the spirit of Hilbert's program.

sign forge

Combine a root with a motion, a light, a tense and a voice. Fuse two roots into one glyph, add a second root to read a relation, refute it, or draw it in your own hand. Every change sounds.

Paste a sign written by you or by a model. Text in [brackets] is ignored.

utterances

Signs in sequence and in layers. A slash moves to the next frame; a bar puts up to five signs in the same frame, one per voice, so several ideas sound and move at once. Load an example, a row from a notebook page, or write your own.

    typed grids

    A page of glyphs typed as Unicode or root codes, one row per line. The reader finds the page's structure, one long cycle or a few base rows in rotation, takes voices and tenses from it, and plays it as a canon: five rows sound together, one column per frame.

    Harmonic: each step reads as its true relation in the harmonic model (contains, converges on, is the dual of, unites with, is distinct from), held at proven. Bare: rule R1 defaults, so each glyph simply is the next.

    your hand

    Every glyph on 31 notebook pages, cut from its grid square, measured and read. The reading is finitary in Hilbert's sense: it counts loops, ends, junctions and parts, and notes where the ink sits and which way it leans. Each glyph gets exactly one reading.

    Families

    Your glyphs fall into 26 families by topology and shape. Each family is the drawn form of one root. The binding below ranks families and roots by complexity: loops, ends, junctions and parts. Rebind any family; the two roots swap. TIM gathers the places where two glyphs ran together side by side, and SYM the places where they stacked.

    Named fusions

    motion

    Motion says how a root relates. Alone it reads as an intransitive verb: infinity converges. Followed by a second root it reads as a verb between them: infinity converges on the limit.

    light

    Sigil light marks how firmly a sign is held. Each light also has its own form and timbre, so the status still reads in grayscale or by ear alone.

    time

    Tense is carried by sound: the pitch contour and loudness envelope of the sign's tone, drawn here as a line whose weight follows the loudness. Negative time writes while erasing; perpendicular time sets a layer beside the others instead of after them.

    voice

    The voice is the animated figure holding the sign. It names who sees, asserts, asks or imagines it, which matters once a person and a model trade signs back and forth.

    SAID-H: Hilbert's program

    Hilbert asked for two things: write mathematics as a formal axiom system, then prove by finitary means, using concrete signs that can be surveyed completely, that the system never derives a contradiction. Gödel showed this cannot be done for any system that contains enough arithmetic. SAID splits along the line Hilbert drew between real and ideal statements. The real part gets a finitary consistency proof; the ideal part gets an honest boundary.

    SAID-R · real

    Every sign built from the 26 roots, 11 motions, 6 lights, 6 times, 5 voices and one optional object: 157,760,460 signs, plus refutation. Finite utterances only.

    Consistency of any finite utterance is decided by a finite computation. The base theory has a finite model. This part of Hilbert's program goes through.

    SAID-I · ideal

    Adds Hilbert's own stroke numerals (UNIT, UNIT UNIT, UNIT UNIT UNIT…), successor as flow, and the unbounded whole WHL over them. INF, WHL and LIM are the ideal roots.

    Once arithmetic can be expressed, Gödel's second theorem applies: SAID-I cannot prove its own consistency. The checker enforces this boundary.

    Formation axioms

    A1
    Signs. A sign is ⟨voice, roots, motion, light, time, object⟩ with one to three roots. Code: @voice ROOT.motion.light.time OBJECT.
    A2
    Content and holding. The content is (roots, motion, object, time). The light says how firmly the content is held and the voice says who holds it, as Frege separated content from the judgment stroke.
    A3
    Refutation. ~c says c is refuted. dissolve is a refuted pulse: "A is not B" is ~"A is B". The false light is a refutation held at proven.
    A4
    Voices. The Doubter's signs are questions and hold at unknown. The Dreamer's hold at most at conjecture. The Witness, Prover and Chorus assert.
    A5
    Time. Two contents meet only at the same time, or when one is eternal. Perpendicular time is its own layer. A sign in negative time erases the same content at every non-eternal time.
    A6
    Quarantine. Paradox light holds a content out of inference. Nothing follows from it, so a contradiction cannot spread.

    Rules of inference

    Premises must be held at conjecture or stronger. A conclusion takes the weakest premise light, capped at proven. A contradiction is c and ~c both held at proven or axiom at times that meet.

    R-REFL
    Every term is itself: A.pulse.axiom.eternal A.
    R-SYM
    pulse, split, merge, orbit and invert turn around: A m B gives B m A, and the same for their refutations.
    R-TRANS
    pulse, contain, expand and flow chain: A m B and B m C give A m C.
    R-CONG
    Identicals substitute: A is B, and anything said of B is said of A.
    R-INV
    The dual of the dual is the same: A invert B and B invert C give A is C.
    R-ANTI
    Mutual containment is identity: A contains B and B contains A give A is B.
    R-SPLIT
    Distinct things are not the same: A split B gives ~(A is B).
    R-EX
    What relates, exists: A m B gives A exists and B exists.
    R-DN
    A refuted refutation is only a conjecture: ~~c gives c at conjecture light. This is the intuitionist reading; double dissolve returns the root only when the root is decided elsewhere.

    Results

    T1The base theory is consistent

    It has a finite model, the harmonic model H. Its domain is the 2,951 root sets. Each root is its harmonic partial h, from 8 to 33. A contains B when h(A) divides h(B), so B is an overtone of A. A is the dual of B when h(A) + h(B) = 41. A gives rise to B when h(A) < h(B). Two things are distinct when they share no prime factor. Every rule instance whose premises hold in H is checked.

    T2Consistency of any finite utterance is decidable

    The closure is finite: at most (terms)² × 10 motions × 6 times × 3 refutation levels facts, and each fact's light only rises, at most four times. The checker returns either a derivation of a contradiction, which is a finite proof object, or the whole closure, which is the least Herbrand model of the utterance and so a certificate of its consistency. The procedure uses bounded loops only, so it is primitive recursive and lives inside PRA, Hilbert's finitary standpoint.

    T3The light algebra is a semilattice

    The joint light of combined signs (rule R4) is the minimum in the order paradox < false < unknown < conjecture < proven < axiom, so it is commutative, associative and idempotent. All 258 cases are checked, with 0 failures.

    T4The Gödel boundary

    SAID-I contains enough arithmetic for Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, so it cannot prove its own consistency sign, NUL+PRF.dissolve.proven.eternal: "a proof of the void dissolves," or no derivation reaches a contradiction. Inside any utterance that uses INF, WHL or LIM, the checker holds that sign at unknown light. In a purely real utterance, the checker's own finite run is the proof, and the sign stays proven.

    T5Relative consistency, after Gentzen

    If SAID-I is read inside Peano arithmetic, its consistency is provable in PRA plus transfinite induction up to ε₀, as in Gentzen's 1936 proof. Signed: @prover NUL+PRF.dissolve.proven.eternal [relative to ε₀], said from outside SAID-I.

    T6Real-soundness

    Hilbert wanted ideal mathematics to be conservative over real mathematics. After Gödel, the defensible goal is real-soundness: ideal reasoning never reaches a real sign that the finite check refutes. SAID enforces it where it is used: every real conclusion of an ideal derivation goes back through the checker.

    T8Harmonic readings are always consistent

    Read a typed grid harmonically and every sign states the true relation between two roots in H: one contains the other, converges on it, is its dual, shares a partial with it, or shares none. Every sign is true in the model, so the whole reading has a model and cannot contradict itself, whatever the grid. The bare reading carries no such guarantee; it only happens to be consistent for both grids here, because identities alone never clash.

    T7Your notebooks, read as chains

    Read row by row, with each glyph acting on the next, almost every row is consistent on its own. Read as whole pages, every page contains contradictions between rows.

    consistency checker

    Paste an utterance, yours or a model's. The checker computes everything that follows, shows any contradiction with its derivation, and if it finds one, quarantines the signs behind it as paradox and checks again.

    rules of combination

    The conventions that let symbols join and still translate to one English reading.

    R1
    A sign is a tuple. Root, motion, light and time, held by a voice. Omitted fields default to @witness, pulse, axiom, now.
    R2
    Fusion overlays roots. Up to three roots joined with + draw as one glyph and sound as one chord of their partials. Named fusions have their own English; unnamed ones read hyphenated.
    R3
    Motion acts to the right. With a second root after it, a motion is a transitive verb; without one, it is intransitive. In a notebook row each glyph acts on the next.
    R4
    The weaker light wins. Joint status is the minimum in paradox < false < unknown < conjecture < proven < axiom (T3).
    R5
    Double dissolve is not free. ~A.dissolve B gives "A is B" only as a conjecture. The root returns at full strength only when it is decided elsewhere (R-DN).
    R6
    Every symbol has a plain-text twin. A model reads the code exactly; a person sees, hears and watches the same sign. Neither form is the translation of the other.
    R7
    Glyphs are drawn, not typed. A hand glyph reads by its topology and placement through the reading key. Same loops, ends, junctions and parts, same family; the meaning holds however the stroke wanders.
    R9
    Typed grids. A grid of bare glyphs is a page. Each glyph acts on the next in its row (R3). Rows that run one cycle are held eternal, with any departure from the cycle in what was; rows that rotate a base row take their tense from the rotation, now for the base, becoming for small shifts ahead, perpendicular for large ones, what was and negative time for shifts behind. Rows sound in groups of five, one voice each.
    R8
    Utterances. / moves to the next frame, | layers up to five signs in one frame, one per voice, [brackets] are ignored, and #p35-0101 cites a notebook glyph (page 35, row 1, column 1) to draw it.
