Electronics
Collage: methods at the same time

A movement can layer several methods, such as Glass's counting chorus over a Feldman field while Cage's chance charts drift through. Each layer keeps its own tempo, key, mode and tuning, and enters and leaves at its own time, so the layers never line up.

Overlapping movements

The next movement begins while the last is still sounding, for 20 seconds to a minute.

Methods and how often they appear

The methods are documented compositional techniques reimplemented from scratch. The music of Cage, Feldman, Babbitt, Eno, Roach, Glass, Riley, Adams, Pärt, Reich and Fripp is under copyright, so none of it was used: their modules, patterns, chords and loops here are newly composed. The learned idioms come only from the public-domain scores listed under Analysis.

Instrument sound

Full uses real string sections with pizzicato and spiccato, a real oboe and timpani, and the percussion, keyboard and world instruments of VSCO 2 CE and VCSL (public domain, CC0). The chorus and soprano sing numbers and solfège: syllables spoken by the Kokoro speech model (Apache 2.0) and made into sung notes with the WORLD vocoder. Each instrument loads the first time a movement needs it. Lighter keeps the smaller solo samples.

Background play

On plays through the browser's media player, with lock-screen and headphone controls, and whenever this page is hidden it lines up about two and a half minutes of music ahead, so slowed or paused background timers can't interrupt it. On a computer it plays alongside your other apps. On an iPhone or iPad, open the page in Safari, start it there, then switch apps; media playback there may pause other apps' audio unless they are set to mix with others.

Abstract random rhythm

When a section turns abstract, some families leave the composed rhythm while the others keep it.

Kinds of rhythm

Tuplet grid: bars redivided into 3–13 equal parts. Stochastic time: random gaps, same order. Aleatoric window: free entries inside two-bar windows. Polytempo: a family loops its material at 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 5:4, 4:3 or 5:7.
